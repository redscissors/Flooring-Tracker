One project, several ways to buy it

When a customer is torn between two or three products, today the job sheet reads as one order with one total. This proposal lets areas belong to options — alternate versions of part of the job — with each option carrying its own subtotal, its own spot on the printed estimate, and its own answer at order-entry time. Area notes come out at the same time.

01

The shape of it

the one decision everything else hangs on

Shared base + options Recommended

Untagged areas are the job everyone gets. Tagged areas are the alternates being compared.

Each option's number is a whole-job number: shared areas + that option's areas, with setting materials consolidated per combination. A job with no tagged areas is exactly today's app — the "hidden Option 1."

Every area picks an option

The alternative: once options exist, each area belongs to exactly one.
Living & HallOption A LaundryOption A Master BathOption A Master BathOption B
Simpler to explain, but Option B's subtotal is just one bathroom — not a number the customer can sign. Comparing whole-job cost means mentally re-adding the shared rooms every time. Passed over for that reason.

The compare flow falls out of the model: right-click an area → Duplicate into Option B copies the area with all its rows, tags the copy, and tags the original Option A. Swap the product on the copy and the comparison exists. Nothing about product rows, materials math, or snapshots changes — an option tag is one field on the area.

A · Compact comparison — everything on one sheet Recommended

The shared job prints exactly as today. The compared areas print inside color-banded option groups, each closing with its own subtotal and the whole-job number. Option extras (grout, mortar) stay inside their option's band as one line — they can't be lumped into the job-wide extras block without double-counting alternates. The single "Estimated total" becomes a three-up comparison the customer can point at.

C · One full sheet per option

Each option prints as a complete, self-contained estimate — shared areas repeated, one grand total, sign the winner. Three options ⇒ three pages. Clearest single document to hand a builder; the worst for side-by-side comparing and triple the paper. Cheap to build (it's the existing sheet run once per option), so it could ship alongside A as a print-time choice: "Compare on one page / Full sheet per option."

The picker appears only when the job actually has options; otherwise the panel opens directly, as today. The choice re-scopes everything downstream — special-order lines, stock SKUs, consolidated grout/mortar quantities, and vendor freight (charged per book over the chosen scope's rows only, so a Glazzio option that loses doesn't ship). The same picker fronts the printed order sheet. "Everything" stays for the rare both-were-ordered job and for eyeballing the complete list.

05

Riding along: area notes come out

The area-note input leaves the area band (its spot is where the option chip now lives) and the note line leaves both print layouts. The field stays in the stored shape and the normalizer — old saved jobs and version snapshots still parse; the text just no longer renders or accepts edits. Product-row notes and project notes are untouched.

06

Open questions

my leanings marked — say the word and any of these flips
1 · Is "shared base + options" the right model?
It makes every option total a whole-job number and keeps un-optioned jobs identical to today. The alternative (every area picks an option) is simpler to explain but produces partial totals. Leaning: shared base.
2 · Do option colors leave the moss palette?
The kit is single-accent by rule, but options need to be tellable apart at a glance — like the tier colors already are. Shown: slate blue / umber / plum, quiet enough to sit on paper. Leaning: distinct hues, as shown.
3 · Right-click only, or chip too?
Right-click is the power move; the option chip on the area band opens the same menu for discoverability and for phones. Leaning: both.
4 · Which print treatment ships?
A (compact comparison) as the default; C (sheet per option) as a print-time toggle since it's nearly free to build; B (matrix) only if you find A too busy for simple one-product comparisons. Leaning: A now, C behind a toggle.
5 · Can options be named?
"Option B" means nothing to a customer; "Marble hex" sells. A rename lives in the right-click menu; unnamed options print as A/B/C. Leaning: yes, optional names.
6 · What do old area notes do?
Keep the data, hide the UI (proposed above) — or show a one-time chip on areas that still carry a note so nothing silently vanishes. Leaning: keep data, hide UI; no chip.
